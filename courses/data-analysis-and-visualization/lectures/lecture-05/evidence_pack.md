## Evidence A: HPS randomized trial

Approximately five years of follow-up. Endpoint: first major vascular event. Each affected participant contributes once to the composite.

| Assigned arm | Participants | With selected endpoint |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Simvastatin | 10,269 | 2,033 |
| Placebo | 10,267 | 2,585 |

Source: HPS Collaborative Group (2002), Figures 3 and 5. Source link: `sources.md`.

1. Calculate each arm's event risk, ARR = control minus treatment, RR = treatment/control and RRR. State units.
2. Translate ARR into fewer affected people per 1,000 during the trial period.
3. Use SE(ARR) = sqrt[pC(1−pC)/nC + pT(1−pT)/nT]. Calculate an approximate 95% interval with ±1.96 SE.
4. A bar chart starts at 15%. Calculate the ratio of visible control/treatment heights, then compare it with the actual risk ratio in that direction.
5. Specify a repaired chart, baseline/reference and caption. State what the randomization directly identifies.

## Evidence B: AIM-HIGH randomized add-on trial

Both groups receive intensive statin therapy. Mean follow-up: three years. Endpoint: first primary composite cardiovascular outcome. Within the niacin arm, median HDL-C rises from 35 to 42 mg/dL at two years.

| Assigned add-on | Participants | With selected endpoint |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Niacin | 1,718 | 282 |
| Placebo | 1,696 | 274 |

Reported HR: 1.02 (95% CI 0.87–1.21). Source: original AIM-HIGH report (2011).

1. Calculate the within-arm change in the HDL median as a percentage. Identify what this quantity measures.
2. Calculate each event risk and their RR.
3. Calculate SE(log RR) = sqrt[1/eT−1/nT+1/eC−1/nC], then exponentiate log RR ±1.96 SE. Compare this count interval with the reported HR interval.
4. Explain why an interval containing 1 does not establish exact equivalence.
5. Sketch separate biomarker and clinical-outcome panels, or a clinical-outcome ratio interval. Write a supported caption.

## Evidence C: NHANES observational cohort

Outcome: CVD mortality. Exposure: modeled usual added-sugar percentage of energy. Q1's 7.4% midvalue is the reference. The numbers are published model estimates, not individual participant records.

| Quintile | Exposure midvalue | Demographic HR (95% CI) | Full HR (95% CI) |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Q1 | 7.4% | 1 reference | 1 reference |
| Q2 | 11.4% | 1.09 (1.05–1.13) | 1.07 (1.02–1.12) |
| Q3 | 14.8% | 1.23 (1.12–1.34) | 1.18 (1.06–1.31) |
| Q4 | 18.7% | 1.49 (1.24–1.78) | 1.38 (1.11–1.70) |
| Q5 | 25.2% | 2.43 (1.63–3.62) | 2.03 (1.26–3.27) |

Demographic adjustment: age, sex, race/ethnicity. Full adjustment also includes education, smoking, alcohol, physical activity, family CVD history, antihypertensive medication, Healthy Eating Index, BMI, systolic blood pressure, total serum cholesterol and total calories. Source: Yang et al. (2014), Table 2.

1. Does 25.2% mark the Q5 category threshold? Q5 starts at 21.3%. Explain the distinction.
2. State the full-model Q5 comparison, outcome and interval. Can it give an absolute mortality probability?
3. A pitch plots only Q1 and Q5 without intervals. Name two visual omissions.
4. Sketch all five estimates on numeric exposure positions and a log HR axis with reference 1. Include intervals; Q1 has no fabricated CI.
5. Explain why full adjustment cannot by itself establish causality or identify a sugar-versus-LDL replacement effect.
