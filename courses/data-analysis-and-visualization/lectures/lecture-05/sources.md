# Sources and extraction record

Verified against primary reports on 7 October 2026. The teaching CSV files are small transcriptions of factual aggregate values. The package does not redistribute full papers or reproduce their original figure artwork. Classroom figures are new visualizations of the extracted evidence.

1. **HPS Collaborative Group (2002).** MRC/BHF Heart Protection Study of cholesterol lowering with simvastatin in 20,536 high-risk individuals: a randomised placebo-controlled trial. *Lancet* 360:7–22. DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(02)09327-3. [Original report PDF](https://vascularsociety.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/landmark%20trials/heart-protection-study.pdf). Figure 3 gives assigned-arm denominators, first major vascular event counts and the logrank event-rate ratio; Figure 5 repeats counts. Extracted: 2033/10269, 2585/10267, reported ratio 0.76 (0.72–0.81). These counts match the saved plan.

2. **AIM-HIGH Investigators (2011).** Niacin in patients with low HDL cholesterol levels receiving intensive statin therapy. *NEJM* 365:2255–2267. DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1107579. [Original report abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085343/). Extracted directly from the original report: 282/1718 versus 274/1696; reported HR 1.02 (0.87–1.21); niacin-arm median HDL 35 to 42 mg/dL at two years. This provides all required counts, so the secondary participant-count paper mentioned in the saved plan is unnecessary for this package's extraction.

3. **Yang et al. (2014).** Added Sugar Intake and Cardiovascular Diseases Mortality Among US Adults. *JAMA Internal Medicine* 174:516–524. DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.13563. [Original paper and Table 2](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1819573). Extracted midvalues: 7.4, 11.4, 14.8, 18.7, 25.2. Demographic HRs/limits: reference 1; 1.09 (1.05–1.13); 1.23 (1.12–1.34); 1.49 (1.24–1.78); 2.43 (1.63–3.62). Full HRs/limits: reference 1; 1.07 (1.02–1.12); 1.18 (1.06–1.31); 1.38 (1.11–1.70); 2.03 (1.26–3.27). Methods establish modeled usual intake, the Cox analysis, survey design and complete adjustment list. Table 2 and Table 3 use different exposure comparisons; their values must not be mixed.

4. **Ference et al. (2017).** Low-density lipoproteins cause atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. 1. Evidence from genetic, epidemiologic, and clinical studies. A consensus statement from the European Atherosclerosis Society Consensus Panel. *European Heart Journal* 38:2459–2472. DOI 10.1093/eurheartj/ehx144. [Evidence synthesis](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/38/32/2459/3745109). Supports the broad LDL causal conclusion. The classroom simvastatin reanalysis has a narrower direct estimand.

5. **Kearns, Schmidt and Glantz (2016).** Sugar Industry and Coronary Heart Disease Research: A Historical Analysis of Internal Industry Documents. *JAMA Internal Medicine* 176:1680–1685. DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.5394. [Original historical analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5099084/). Opening motivation only: sponsor involvement and undisclosed review funding. This source supplies no medical effect estimate used in the lecture.

## Separation of evidence and teaching examples

- All trial counts, HDL medians and sugar HRs are sourced facts.
- Risk differences, count RRs, their approximate intervals, baseline ratios and per-1,000 translations are classroom calculations from those facts.
- Diet examples (50 g/2000 kcal and 100 g/4000 kcal) and optional area values (1 and 2) are explicitly invented arithmetic exercises.
- The opening pitches are invented editor briefs. They are not quotations or claims attributed to the researchers.
- Equal-denominator dot arrays scale observed trial risks and round to whole dots. They do not depict identified or reconstructed patients.

The class teaches evidence interpretation rather than a review of current treatment guidelines.
